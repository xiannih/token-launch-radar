How the value is worked out — fitted, not guessed

Expected value = TVL × category multiple × size × growth × age × token odds. The category multiple is the median market-cap-per-TVL of launched protocols in the same category ( across all of them), shrunk toward that global median for small samples. The size term is a fitted log-log slope (): big protocols trade at far lower multiples. Growth is fitted too, and the snapshot says growing paid nothing (), so it stays ×1.00. Age is fitted as well ( a year): younger protocols trade at higher multiples, so age is a small penalty, not a bonus. Token odds is the share of the protocol's peers — same category, same age band — that already record a token ( of all protocols have one). The implied value is therefore an expected value: what a launch would be worth, times the chance a protocol like it launches at all.

Hold-out test. The launched protocols with a positive market cap are split in half by slug; the terms are fitted on one half and used to predict the other's market caps. Median miss × one way and × the other, against × and × for a global-median-only baseline. The fit explains little — market-cap-per-TVL is noisy, and launched protocols record a market cap of $0 or less and are left out of the fit entirely. The board is a ranking by the fitted median, never a price forecast, and never a prediction of whether or when a token launches.

Protocols that already record a token are excluded outright — no penalty can place a $100B token protocol honestly below a $5M pre-token one on a value-ordered board. The excluded ones are listed below with their ratios, so the separation is visible. Eligible means: no token recorded, not a wrapped, staked or bridged product, not an exchange, not a versioned copy.
